# 未見文 第 30 群（第 525 組）: 入試長文に近い自作の段落 2 本（都市の街路樹の説明文 15 文・財布をなくした体験の物語 15 文）を 1 文ずつ
Trees in cities do much more than make streets look beautiful.
On hot summer days, they provide shade and help keep the air cooler.
In fact, streets lined with trees can be several degrees cooler than those without them.
Trees also absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen, which helps improve air quality.
Some studies have even found that people who live near green spaces tend to feel less stressed.
Despite these benefits, many cities have been cutting down trees to make room for roads and buildings.
Once a large tree is removed, it can take decades for a new one to grow to the same size.
To deal with this problem, some local governments have started programs to plant more trees.
In one city, residents can ask the city to plant a tree in front of their homes for free.
The residents, in turn, agree to water the young tree during its first few summers.
Such programs not only increase the number of trees but also encourage people to care about their neighborhoods.
However, planting trees is not as simple as it might seem.
If the wrong kind of tree is chosen, its roots may damage sidewalks or underground pipes.
Experts therefore recommend choosing species that are suited to the local climate and soil.
With careful planning, trees can make our cities healthier and more pleasant places to live.
Last spring, I left my wallet on a train on my way to a job interview.
I did not notice it was missing until I tried to buy a drink at the station.
My heart sank when I realized that my money, my student ID, and my train pass were all gone.
Since I had no time to go back, I borrowed some money from a friend and went to the interview anyway.
All through the interview, I kept worrying about what I would do if the wallet was never found.
That evening, I received a phone call from the railway company.
A woman told me that someone had handed in a wallet with my name on it.
When I went to the office to pick it up, nothing had been taken.
I asked who had found it, but the staff said the person had left without giving a name.
I was deeply moved that a stranger had gone out of their way to help me.
Since then, I have tried to be more careful with my belongings.
More importantly, the experience made me want to help others in the same way.
A few weeks later, I found an umbrella left on a bench and took it to the lost-and-found office.
It was a small thing, but I hoped it would make someone's day a little better.
Kindness, I learned, is often passed from one person to another.
