# 第 38 群（段落 2 本・30 文）と修正の変種（第 533 組）
On a hot summer afternoon, the center of a large city can be several degrees warmer than the countryside around it.
This phenomenon, known as the urban heat island effect, has become a serious problem in many parts of the world.
One of its main causes is the materials used to build roads and buildings.
Concrete and asphalt absorb heat from the sun during the day and release it slowly at night, which keeps temperatures high even after sunset.
In addition, cities have far fewer trees and green spaces than rural areas, so there is less shade and less water evaporating from plants to cool the air.
Heat produced by cars, air conditioners, and factories makes the situation even worse.
Higher temperatures are not just uncomfortable; they can also be dangerous, especially for elderly people and small children.
During heat waves, hospitals in some cities have reported a sharp increase in the number of patients suffering from heatstroke.
Moreover, as people use more air conditioning to stay cool, the demand for electricity rises, which in turn leads to more greenhouse gas emissions.
Fortunately, there are several ways to reduce the effect.
Some cities have started painting roofs white so that they reflect sunlight instead of absorbing it.
Others are planting trees along streets and creating green roofs covered with grass and small plants.
Even small parks can lower the temperature of the surrounding neighborhood by a degree or two.
Experts believe that if these measures are combined, cities could become much more comfortable places to live.
However, they also point out that such changes take time and require cooperation between governments, businesses, and residents.

I was surprised to find out how little I knew about the place where I had grown up.
My first group was a family from Australia with two young children who kept asking questions.
At first I was so nervous that I spoke too quickly, and the father had to ask me to repeat several things.
Then I remembered my teacher's advice to relax and pretend I was talking to a friend.
After that, the tour went much more smoothly, and the children even laughed at one of my jokes.
When we reached the top of the castle, the mother told me that the view was the most beautiful one she had seen in Japan.
At the end of the tour, the family thanked me and gave me a small koala keychain as a present.
Since then, I have guided more than twenty groups from various countries.
Not every tour has gone perfectly, but each one has taught me something new.
I have learned that communication is not only about using correct grammar but also about listening carefully and showing interest in others.
Through this experience, I have also come to appreciate my own town much more than before.
I hope to continue working as a guide until I graduate, and someday I would like to show visitors around other parts of Japan as well.
