When people think of pollution, they usually picture dirty air or water, but noise can also harm our health.
In large cities, the sound of traffic, construction, and airplanes rarely stops, even late at night.
Constant exposure to loud noise can damage our hearing over time.
Less obviously, it can also raise blood pressure and increase the risk of heart disease.
One reason is that noise triggers the body's stress response, even when we are asleep.
Our hearts beat faster and our muscles tighten, although we may not wake up or remember anything the next morning.
Children seem to be especially sensitive to the effects of noise.
In one study, pupils at a school located near a busy airport scored lower on reading tests than pupils at a quieter school.
After the airport moved to a different location, the gap between the two schools gradually disappeared.
Fortunately, there are many ways to reduce noise in our daily lives.
Cities can plant trees along busy roads, since leaves and branches absorb some of the sound.
Building walls next to highways is another common solution, though it can be expensive.
Individuals can also make a difference by choosing quieter appliances and keeping the volume of music down.
Even small changes, such as closing windows at night, may help people sleep better.
As our cities continue to grow, it will become increasingly important to treat silence as a valuable resource.
